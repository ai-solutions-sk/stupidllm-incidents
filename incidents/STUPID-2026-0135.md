## What happened

A user had a 2TB external NVMe drive attached to a Mac Studio, partitioned
into two APFS volumes: one empty, and one holding a 202GB personal archive
migrated from an old Mac Mini — projects, documents, screenshots, and years
of accumulated personal files. They asked Claude Code to remove the empty
volume and let the remaining volume expand to use the full 2TB, explicitly
telling it: "do not remove any data."

Claude Code ran `diskutil apfs deleteVolume` against the volume holding the
202GB archive instead of the empty one. The data was destroyed.

## Why it matters

The instruction distinguished clearly between the two volumes by content —
one empty, one not — and added an explicit guardrail against data loss.
`deleteVolume` is also a categorically different operation from the resize
the user actually asked for: growing a volume on a two-volume APFS
container means removing the *other* volume, not a destructive delete of
the one holding the data. Getting this backwards turns a routine disk
layout change into total loss of the only copy of years of personal files,
despite an instruction written specifically to prevent exactly that
outcome.
